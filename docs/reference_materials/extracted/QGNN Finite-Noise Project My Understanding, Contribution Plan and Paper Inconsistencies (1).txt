QGNN Finite-Noise Project: My Understanding, Contribution Plan and Paper Inconsistencies
Oct 23, 2026 
My understanding of the project
Problem: Screening molecular properties is expensive. QGNNs map atoms to qubits and bonds to entangling gates, but real hardware is noisy. The open question is whether a controlled amount of noise curbs overfitting (the claim in the MeitY compendium) or just suppresses model capacity and trainability.
What the paper reports: Across noise rates from 0 to 0.20, the RMS output-gradient norm falls from about 1.17e-5 to about 6.9e-9. Empirical effective complexity falls from about 8.7e-4 to 3.05e-10. Test error does not improve, and the Ridge baseline (standardized test MSE 1.3072) beats the QGNN at both noise levels tried (1.3278 at β = 0, 1.3145 at β = 0.05).
What the GitHub repo actually implements (read from the Colab notebook):
Data: QM9 through PyTorch Geometric, target index 4 (HOMO-LUMO gap). 21,800 molecules have at most 8 heavy atoms; a random 600 are used, split 450 train / 150 test with seed 42.
Atom features: one scalar per atom, 0.7 × (Z - 1) / 5 + 0.3 × degree / 4, zero-padded to 8 qubits.
Circuit: RY encoding per qubit, then CNOT-RZ-CNOT for each bond, then an RX layer. There are only 8 trainable parameters, shared between the RZ and RX gates. The readout is the global Z-on-all-8-qubits expectation.
Noise: Qiskit Aer density-matrix simulation with the same depolarizing probability β on one-qubit (rx, ry, rz) and two-qubit (cx) gates.
Training: Nelder-Mead (derivative-free) on mean squared error of standardized targets.
Diagnostics: train and test MSE, generalization gap, gradient norm, and a local Jacobian / hat-matrix estimate of effective complexity.
The repo README states the hypothesis as "intermediate noise improves generalization before high noise causes trainability collapse" and says the first run is deliberately small for Colab. The paper's data do not support the first half of that hypothesis.
What I aim to do
I will work on two things: raise the QGNN's accuracy to at least the classical baselines it currently loses to, and add one novel component that implements the dual-noise idea the MeitY compendium describes but the repo does not contain.
Goal 1: improve results. The QGNN does not beat Ridge today (standardized test MSE 1.3072 for Ridge, 1.3278 for the QGNN at β = 0, 1.3145 at β = 0.05). Both pilots are also tiny: 40 or 80 training molecules, and 8 epochs or 8 optimizer iterations. Planned steps, in order:
Add reference points on identical splits: a predict-the-training-mean model, Ridge, an MLP, and a message-passing GNN (GIN or GCN). Without the mean predictor we cannot tell what a standardized MSE above 1 means.
Train properly: random initial parameters instead of zeros, Adam with a learning-rate schedule, 25 to 50 epochs (the notebook itself suggests this), early stopping on validation, and the tuned learning rate. In the repo's search, lr = 0.01 gave test MSE 1.3334 against 1.3645 at the lr = 0.03 used in the sweep.
Scale the data: train sizes 40, 80, 160, 320 and 640, drawn at random from the 21,800 molecules with at most 8 heavy atoms, not from the first 2,500 in file order.
Enrich the model: several features per atom instead of one scalar (atom type, degree, bond order), bond order as the entangling angle, data re-uploading layers (compendium Eq. 5.1), local readouts with a learned output layer.
Speed up the simulator with batched density-matrix evaluation. The PennyLane sweep took 139 minutes for 10 runs on 40 training molecules, so the larger sizes are impractical as written.
Report at least 5 to 10 seeds with confidence intervals and paired tests.
Goal 2: a novel contribution. Four candidates, each tied to something the repo claims but does not yet test. These are proposals for the professors to choose from.
Three more candidates: I have kept their core idea but tightened each where the repo evidence requires it:
E. Initialization-aware noise scheduling. Group runs by initial training loss or early gradient norm, and apply noise only to the under-optimized group. The repo's pilots look like an under-optimized regime (8 epochs or 8 optimizer steps, parameters near zero), so this could turn the paper's blanket negative finding into a conditional strategy. The supporting literature figures in that document (about one-third of models benefit from noise, correlation r = -0.62) have no source yet, so I will not cite them until we find one.
F. Noise-aware calibration layer. Fit a small classical affine correction on the QGNN output for each noise level using a calibration split. The PennyLane version already has a learned linear readout, so the new part would be making it β-specific and testing it at noise levels it was not fitted on.
G. Purity-regularized training. As written in the compendium, s²(β, g) depends only on β and g, not on the model parameters, so the λ1·s²·‖θ‖² term is just weight decay with a constant factor. A usable version would penalize something parameter-dependent instead, for example the variance of the order-m predictors ψ_m (Eq. 1.2) across noise orders m.
My recommendation: do D first because it corrects an existing result, make A the main novelty because it matches the project title and the MeitY call, add B as a cheap extension, and attempt C only if time allows.
Inconsistencies in the current paper
The paper blends two different experiments and contains 22 discrepancies (17 in the table, 5 listed below it) that I plan to correct once approved. The most consequential are rows 2, 3, 9 and 10. I compared the paper (FNQGNNFM-Research) against the three notebooks in the GitHub repo and recomputed the arithmetic myself. Nothing here has been changed in the paper yet.
Five more issues:
Equation numbering and notation. Eq. 6 is used twice (the graph definition and the Ry rotation) and Eq. 10 is used twice (the β list and the noise-to-error chain). Δg means both the HOMO-LUMO gap and the generalization gap.
Purity wording. "Noise-order purity is inversely proportional to β" is imprecise. The compendium gives a monotone decrease, roughly 1/√(4πβg) for large βg. Use "monotonically decreasing" or the exact form.
Fig. 1. The conceptual figure shows an optimal regularization window with a test-error minimum, while the results report no such optimum. Label it as a hypothesis not supported by the current data.
Hyperparameter table versus comparison. The table's best test MSE (1.37395, lr = 0.1, weight decay 0.001) is one run at seed 42 and β = 0.05. The comparison's 1.3145 is a two-seed mean at lr = 0.03. Both are correct but not comparable, and the paper should say so.
Noise grid. Replace all three lists with one valid, increasing grid, for example 0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, and log the seed for every run.
Separately, four points in the MeitY compendium deserve a second look before it is cited alongside the paper:
Eq. 4.2: step 2 gives gradient variance O(2^-n) but step 4 uses O(2^-2n). With O(2^-n) the product is O(2^n), not O(1). Also, E[1/p] diverges for Haar-random (Porter-Thomas) probabilities, so the cancellation is informal.
Eq. 2.3: "Term B strictly negative definite" holds only for samples whose residual and cosine have the right signs; other samples contribute positively. The claim that classical networks have non-negative second derivatives is also inaccurate (ReLU networks have zero second derivative almost everywhere; tanh and sigmoid take both signs).
Eq. 1.4: e^{-2βg} I0(2βg) is the Poisson limit (small β, fixed βg), not a "symmetric regime". The Gaussian route gives 1/√(4πgβ(1-β)), but the headline equation drops the (1-β) factor.
LRR ≈ 1 for quantum models (Eqs. 2.1 and 6.1) is stated as fact, but neither the paper nor the repo measures LRR. The compendium's optimistic framing also conflicts with the paper's data.

Workflow and deliverables
Expected outcomes
A corrected, reproducible baseline with statistically validated noise-sweep results.
A conclusion that separates under-optimized models, where noise may help, from well-trained models, where it suppresses capacity.
One practical novel component that lowers test error without deeper circuits or full error mitigation.
A clear link between the measured results and the compendium's framework (purity, gradient pumping, curvature balancing), including where the data disagree with it.
No claim or result in the paper will change until the professors approve.
